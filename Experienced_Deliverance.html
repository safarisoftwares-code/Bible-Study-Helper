⚔️ Experienced Deliverance

← Back
On Thursday, 26th November 2026, at JCK (Jerusalem City – Kenya) Prayer Centre, under the Chief Commander, Apostle James Maina Ng'ang'a, our spiritual father, we went out to a field that hosts prayers and grazes the institution's animals. The day's subject was Kung'oa Weeds — uprooting. We carried timber and stones, and we pulled weeds. The day taught us something about deliverance that a classroom could not: how roots hold, how some give way at a touch and some need many hands, and how a field is never cleared in one visit. This is what we saw, and what it taught us.

The Field

We came to a field that belongs to prayer. Its purpose is not only to feed animals; it is ground set apart for seeking God. That is where the lesson began — deliverance, too, is work done on ground that belongs to God, under authority, not on our own initiative.

The sun was up. The timber and the stones were carried. The weeds came up one by one. Some came away with a single pull. Some needed two or three of us leaning in together. Some needed a man to kneel and gather all his strength before the root would give. By the time we left, the field was not finished — the day had run out before the work did.

And as each weed came out of the ground, we understood: you uproot a weed here, and by the power of God a problem is uprooted there.

What the Weeds Taught Us About Deliverance

Some weeds come out at the first pull

Some spirits leave at the first worship. Before a word is spoken against them, the presence of God moves and they go. We should not be surprised, and we should not take credit. The same power that loosens a shallow root loosens a shallow hold. John 16:8 ↗

Some weeds have deep roots

Others would not move at a touch. The root had gone down, and pulling the leaf did nothing. So it is with spirits that have settled in a life over years. They do not leave because a prayer was said once. Mark 9:29 ↗ Deep roots call for deeper seeking.

Some weeds need two or three together

Some roots would not give to one man alone. Two or three leaned in, and together the root came free. Deliverance is not a solo work. Ecclesiastes 4:12 ↗ Christ gave authority to His church, and He sends us together (Luke 10:1).

Some weeds need a man on his knees

There were weeds that only came out when a man knelt and concentrated his strength. Kneeling is not weakness; it is gathering power for the pull. James 4:10 ↗ Fasting and prayer are the kneeling of the spirit (Mark 9:29).

The field is not finished in one day

We left before the field was done, and that is the way of deliverance. God told Israel He would drive out their enemies little by little — not because He lacked power, but so the land would not be left desolate and the person worse than before. Exodus 23:29–30 ↗ Philippians 1:6 ↗ Deliverance is a journey, not a single event.

Once you begin to pull a weed, you must finish it

A half-pulled weed takes root again, and a half-cast-out spirit returns with others worse than himself. Matthew 12:43–45 ↗ Luke 11:24–26 ↗ That is why a work once begun must be completed. We did not leave a weed half out of the ground, and deliverance must not be left half done.

Every weed pulled here, a problem uprooted there

This was the lesson of the day, and it is the promise of God: Jeremiah 1:10 ↗ The servant who pulls weeds in the field is the same servant who, by the power of God, sees problems uprooted from a life. The work in the field and the work in the spirit are one work.

Where the Comparison Stops

A field is a picture, not a doctrine. A weed is not a demon, and a cleared field does not guarantee a delivered soul. Two things must be said plainly:

  • The authority is Christ's, not ours. We do not cast out by our own strength, our own fasting, or our own name. Luke 10:19 ↗ — the authority is given, and it is His (Matthew 10:1 ↗).
  • The power is God's, and He gives the growth. 1 Corinthians 3:6–9 ↗ We pull; God uproots.

Where the picture helps, use it. Where it fails, Scripture is the authority, not the field.

Application

For the one being delivered: Do not be discouraged if the work takes longer than a day. God drives out little by little, and He finishes what He begins. Submit to those He has set over you, and keep seeking Him.

For the servants who pray: Serve with joy — the field is the Lord's, and the work is glad work. Pray with persistence; some roots need many hands and many knees. Work together; a cord of three strands is not quickly broken. And finish what you begin — a work once started must be completed, for the sake of the one being set free.
